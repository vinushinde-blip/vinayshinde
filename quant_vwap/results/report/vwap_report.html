Quantitative research note

VWAP mean​-reversion across the NSE 500

How far intraday price wanders from session VWAP, how often, what happens next — and whether trading the snap-back is worth the stop-loss. Built on two years of 1‑, 5‑ and 15‑minute bars for all 500 NSE 500 constituents, fetched live from Zerodha Kite Connect.

01

Scope & method

What was measured, over what universe, and how VWAP and “delta%” are defined.

Universe & data

All 500 NSE 500 index constituents (current membership, applied retroactively — see Limitations). 1‑minute, 5‑minute and 15‑minute OHLCV candles, 17 Aug 2024 → 17 Aug 2026, pulled live via the Kite Connect historical-data API (chunked at each interval’s per-request limit: 60/100/200 days).

Session VWAP

Recomputed from scratch every session (VWAP never carries across days). Typical price (H+L+C)/3, volume-weighted, cumulative from the day’s open.

Delta% & ranges

delta% = (close − vwap) / vwap × 100. Four thresholds tested both above and below VWAP: 1.5% · 2% · 3% · 4%.

A crossing event fires the instant delta% moves from inside a band to outside it — e.g. price steps from +1.3% to +1.6% away from VWAP, crossing the 1.5% threshold. Every stock × timeframe × threshold × direction combination is tracked independently across the full two years.

Reversion behavior

Once delta% crosses a threshold, does price actually come back to VWAP — and how fast? “Full reversion” means delta% touches zero again within the window; “band exit” only means it drifts back inside the threshold.

Note the timeframe effect: on 1‑minute bars a 1.5% deviation reverts fully only ~4–6% of the time within 20 bars; on 15‑minute bars the same threshold reverts ~54–66% of the time — a slow-bar deviation is a much stronger reversion signal than a fast-bar one.

Volume confirms, it doesn’t contradict. Reversion is consistently more likely, not less, when the crossing happens on above-average volume (high vol_ratio) — a high-volume 1.5% overshoot on 15‑min bars exits the band 94% of the time vs. 90% on low volume. A volume spike accompanying a VWAP deviation reads as exhaustion, not continuation.
04

Strategy rules

The exact mean-reversion rule set that was backtested — every stock, every timeframe, every threshold, both target:stop variants.

  1. Trigger. Delta% crosses further from VWAP through a threshold (1.5% / 2% / 3% / 4%).
  2. Direction. Price above VWAP by ≥ threshold → short, betting on reversion down. Price below VWAP by ≥ threshold → long, betting on reversion up.
  3. Entry. Next bar’s open (no lookahead on the signal bar).
  4. Target : stop-loss. Two variants backtested in parallel — 1.0% : 0.5% and 1.5% : 0.5%, both measured off entry price.
  5. Exit priority. First of target hit, stop hit, or end-of-session square-off. A bar that touches both levels is resolved as stop-loss-first (conservative).
  6. No overnight risk. VWAP resets every session, so every open position is forced flat at the day’s last bar — positions never carry to the next day.
  7. One position at a time per stock × timeframe × threshold × direction × variant. A new signal while a trade is open is skipped, not stacked.

The edge concentrates in one corner: 1‑minute bars, 3–4% thresholds, the wider 1.5%:0.5% target. That combination has few trades (1,000–14,000 vs. 40,000–100,000 at 1.5%) but a real edge — profit factor 1.07–1.21. Fifteen-minute bars are the mirror image: unprofitable almost everywhere, worst on the “below VWAP, buy the dip” side, where a 4% deviation costs −0.11% expectancy per trade. On slow bars, a large VWAP deviation reads as trend, not exhaustion — consistent with the reversion-probability finding in Section 03.
06

Market context: does the Nifty regime matter?

Nifty 50 carries no traded volume in Kite’s feed, so it gets a price/momentum-only regime tag — strong_up / strong_down / neutral, from EMA(50), ADX(14) and MACD histogram — instead of its own VWAP. The nifty_filtered variant skips long entries while the index is in strong_down, and short entries while it’s in strong_up.

Filtering out the “wrong-way” regime doesn’t clearly help

Directional return, 1.5% threshold — positive means price moved toward VWAP

Counter-intuitive finding: buying an oversold-vs-VWAP stock (“below”, long) while the Nifty is in a strong_down regime reverted more, not less — 20‑bar directional return of +4.3% vs. +0.6% in a neutral tape. Sharp broad-market selloffs seem to produce sharper individual-stock snap-backs, not weaker ones. A blanket “don’t buy dips in a downtrend” filter removes some of the best trades in the baseline set — the aggregate nifty_filtered numbers in Section 05 are net worse than baseline on the long side for exactly this reason. Where the filter does earn its keep is the mirror case: shorting an overbought stock while the index itself is strong_up is the one regime/direction pairing worth avoiding on priors, and the data is directionally consistent with that, if not dramatically so.
07

Deployable strategy — ₹10L backtest

One fully-specified rule set, sized against ₹10,00,000 of capital, with position sizing, a concurrency cap, and real transaction costs — not just the per-trade % this study used everywhere else.

Of every cell tested, 1‑minute bars, ±4% delta from VWAP, both directions, 1.5%:0.5% target:stop has the best gross risk-adjusted profile: the highest Sharpe (3.10) and the smallest drawdown (−3.3%) of any configuration in Section 05, and more trades (7,602) than either direction alone — which matters, because at 3,000–4,600 trades a single-direction result is more exposed to sample noise. It is also the simplest rule to actually state: fade any 4%+ move away from VWAP, in either direction.

Rule set

Universe
NSE 500 constituents (425 of 500 produced at least one signal over the 2‑year backtest).
Timeframe
1‑minute candles, session VWAP (typical price, resets daily).
Entry trigger
delta% crosses ±4% away from VWAP — above → short, below → long.
Entry price
Next 1‑minute bar’s open after the signal (no same-bar fill).
Target
1.5% in the reversion direction from entry.
Stop-loss
0.5% against the reversion direction from entry.
Exit priority
Target → stop → end-of-session square-off, whichever comes first. Same-bar ambiguity resolved stop-first.
Position size
10% of current capital per trade (compounds with realized P&L), share quantity rounded down.
Concurrency cap
Max 10 open positions at once (≤100% of capital deployed). A new signal is skipped, not queued, if all 10 slots are full — happened to 96 of 7,698 signals (1.2%).
Leverage
None modeled — every position is backed 1:1 by capital. MIS/short-sell margin from your broker would allow more concurrent slots than shown here.
Costs
Brokerage + STT + exchange charges + GST + stamp duty, bundled as one round-trip % of trade notional — run at two scenarios below.

Honest verdict: this edge is real but thin, and the cost assumption decides the outcome. At an optimistic 0.05% round-trip cost, ₹10L compounds to ₹10.45L over 2 years (+2.2% CAGR, Sharpe 0.37, max drawdown −10.0%) — a real but unremarkable return for the drawdown taken. At a more realistic 0.08% round-trip cost (typical discount-broker brokerage + STT + exchange/SEBI charges + GST + stamp duty on ~7,600 round-trips), the same signals compound to ₹8.33L (−8.8% CAGR, Sharpe −1.38). The gross statistical edge documented in Sections 02–06 is genuine, but it is not, on these numbers, large enough to clear realistic NSE intraday transaction costs at this trade frequency. Making it deployable would require cutting cost-per-trade (larger position sizes to dilute the flat ₹20 brokerage floor, a lower-cost broker, or fewer/likelier-to-win signals) rather than the rule set itself.
08

Limitations

Costs: modeled only in Section 07

Sections 02–06 report gross, cost-free per-trade %, as does Section 05’s heatmap. Only the ₹10L strategy backtest in Section 07 deducts brokerage/STT/exchange/GST/stamp duty — and a single blended round-trip % is still an approximation, not a per-charge model. No slippage is modeled anywhere.

Fill assumptions

Entries fill exactly at next-bar open; target/stop fill exactly at the level. Same-bar target+stop ambiguity is resolved stop-first (conservative but not certain). No partial fills or liquidity constraints.

Survivorship & universe

Today’s NSE 500 membership was applied across the full two-year window; stocks that entered or left the index mid-period aren’t reconstructed historically.

Shorting mechanics

“Short” trades assume intraday short-sell is available and frictionless for every stock. In practice this needs margin/MIS and is subject to exchange short-sell restrictions on some names.

Compounding: modeled only in Section 07

Sections 02–06 report per-trade % only. Section 07 compounds equity and caps concurrency at 10 positions, but still ignores correlation risk — e.g. a broad-market gap can trigger many simultaneous VWAP-deviation signals that are not as independent as the position count suggests.

Regime tag is coarse

The Nifty regime classifier (EMA50/ADX/MACD) is a simple heuristic, not a validated regime model — treat Section 06 as directional evidence, not a tuned filter.
